# Writing Rules

These are practical controlled-English rules inspired by ASD-STE100. They do not replace the official specification or its controlled vocabulary.

## Terminology

- Use one preferred term for each concept.
- Do not change an official product, API, command, schema, event, field, or UI name.
- Define uncommon domain terms before repeated use when the audience may not know them.
- Avoid unnecessary synonyms because they can suggest that two concepts are different.

## Sentences

- Put one main action or idea in each sentence.
- Prefer active voice when it makes the actor clearer.
- Keep the subject close to the verb.
- Avoid long chains of dependent clauses.
- Avoid ambiguous `it`, `this`, `that`, `they`, or `which` when two or more referents are possible.

## Procedures

- Use the imperative for direct actions.
- Put a required condition before the action that depends on it.
- Put prerequisite steps before dependent steps.
- Use explicit warnings or prohibitions when omission could cause damage, data loss, security risk, or an invalid result.
- Keep one primary action in each numbered step when practical.

## Word choice

Prefer short common words when meaning is unchanged.

| Avoid when unnecessary | Prefer |
| --- | --- |
| utilize | use |
| in order to | to |
| due to the fact that | because |
| at this point in time | now |
| make a determination | determine / check |
| perform a restart | restart |
| has the ability to | can |

Do not simplify a specialized technical term if the replacement becomes less precise.

## House length targets

The default 80% mode uses these review targets:

- procedural sentence: aim for 20 words or fewer;
- descriptive sentence: aim for 25 words or fewer;
- paragraph: keep one clear topic and split it when the topic changes.

Do not split a sentence if the split hides an important logical relationship.

## Punctuation and structure

- Prefer simple sentence punctuation.
- Use lists when several parallel conditions or actions are easier to scan than one sentence.
- Use parentheses sparingly.
- Avoid decorative headings and unnecessary rhetorical language in technical procedures.
