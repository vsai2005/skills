# Voice and Genre

## Voice reference mode

Use 2-3 representative paragraphs when possible. Prefer samples written for a similar audience and purpose.

Capture observable tendencies only:

- direct versus scene-setting openings;
- short/medium/long sentence mix;
- paragraph density;
- formality;
- contractions;
- first-person and second-person use;
- question frequency;
- explicit transition frequency;
- punctuation habits;
- vocabulary complexity.

Do not imitate misspellings, factual errors, or unhealthy habits merely because they occur in the sample.

Do not infer protected or hidden personal traits.

## Genre profiles

### Email or chat

- get to the point quickly;
- keep paragraphs short;
- preserve the sender's real level of warmth;
- avoid corporate filler unless the organization requires it.

### Engineering update or release note

- lead with what changed or what matters;
- prefer evidence over praise;
- preserve exact product, version, metric, and compatibility details;
- use technical terminology consistently.

### Technical explanation

- optimize for comprehension before style;
- introduce necessary context, not a generic introduction;
- keep examples concrete;
- do not simplify away important uncertainty or constraints.

### Report or executive update

- preserve a professional tone;
- make decisions, evidence, risks, and next actions easy to find;
- avoid decorative enthusiasm.

### Public post or LinkedIn-style prose

- preserve the author's real point of view;
- use a strong opening only when the source supports it;
- avoid manufactured vulnerability, fake lessons, or invented personal anecdotes;
- do not force every post into hook -> three bullets -> inspirational conclusion.

### Academic or research prose

- preserve hedging, citations, and claim strength;
- do not replace precise technical language merely to sound casual;
- naturalness comes from clear argument flow, not informality.

## Voice priority

When evidence conflicts, use this order:

1. factual and technical integrity;
2. explicit user instructions;
3. authentic voice sample;
4. source voice;
5. genre convention;
6. generic naturalness heuristics.
