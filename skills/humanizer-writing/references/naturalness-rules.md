# Naturalness Rules

Use these rules as editing guidance, not as a mechanical scoring formula.

## Voice

- Identify the audience, purpose, and existing level of formality first.
- Preserve first person only when the source already uses it or the author explicitly wants it.
- Do not add personality traits that are not present in the source.
- Keep confidence calibrated to the evidence. Avoid converting `may` into `will` or `observed` into `proved`.

## Rhythm

- Vary sentence length when the ideas naturally differ in complexity.
- Avoid three or more consecutive sentences with the same opening structure.
- Avoid making every paragraph the same length.
- Use contractions only when they fit the audience and original voice.
- Prefer normal punctuation. Do not add decorative em dashes, ellipses, or fragments simply to appear informal.

## Specificity

- Prefer the concrete subject already available in the source.
- Prefer measured results over generic praise.
- Prefer direct verbs over abstract noun phrases.
- Remove adjectives that add tone but no information.

## Transitions

Use explicit transitions only when the relationship is not obvious from sentence order.

Avoid repetitive connectors such as:

- Furthermore
- Moreover
- Additionally
- Consequently
- In conclusion
- Overall

These words are not forbidden. Use them only when they genuinely improve the logic.

## Structure

- Start close to the point instead of with a generic scene-setting paragraph.
- Do not add a conclusion that merely repeats the introduction.
- Keep headings only when they help navigation.
- Do not force every explanation into a three-part formula.

## Boundaries

Natural writing is not deliberately imperfect writing. Do not introduce misspellings, grammar errors, false starts, fabricated anecdotes, or unsupported opinions to simulate a person.
