# Humanization Review Checklist

## Need for change

- [ ] The source was audited before rewriting.
- [ ] If the source was already natural and clear, unnecessary rewriting was avoided.
- [ ] The selected Light / Standard / Deep intensity matches the actual problem.

## Meaning

- [ ] Facts, numbers, dates, requirements, warnings, and uncertainty are unchanged.
- [ ] Technical identifiers, commands, labels, citations, URLs, and quoted text remain exact.
- [ ] Negation and modal strength (`may`, `must`, `should`, `will`) were preserved.
- [ ] No new claim, anecdote, result, source, opinion, or personal experience was invented.
- [ ] The final candidate was checked against the original source, not only against an intermediate rewrite.

## Voice

- [ ] The tone matches the audience, purpose, and genre.
- [ ] If a real voice sample exists, observable style tendencies were used without copying phrases or inventing traits.
- [ ] The rewrite preserves the author's actual point of view and level of formality.
- [ ] Informality, contractions, humor, or first-person language were not added without evidence.

## Naturalness

- [ ] The text starts near the real point.
- [ ] Sentence and paragraph rhythm is not mechanically repetitive.
- [ ] Generic filler and empty praise are removed.
- [ ] Transitions follow the logic instead of a template.
- [ ] Concrete source facts replace vague claims when possible.
- [ ] Normal punctuation and useful three-item lists were not removed merely because they can be overused by models.

## Long-form continuity

- [ ] Terminology is stable across sections.
- [ ] Voice and claim strength remain consistent.
- [ ] Open threads and references were not lost.

## Integrity

- [ ] No deliberate typos or awkwardness were introduced.
- [ ] The rewrite does not claim human authorship or AI-detector evasion.
- [ ] If `asd-ste100-writing` also applies, terminology, warnings, conditions, and technical precision remain intact.
